# data

The dataset build ([spec #20](../docs/specs/20.md) «Набор данных»): the builder that turns an OSM extract into the road graph and its build report, the zone outlines, the cities file, the places file, the commands around them, and the monthly world build in GitHub Actions that puts the dataset in R2.

## Builder

`buildGraph` in `src/build-graph.ts` reads an OSM file (`src/osm/`: XML for the test fixtures, PBF for extracts) and returns the graph in the format of `engine/src/graph.ts`.

- It picks the roads by their tags (`src/roads.ts`), so a raw Geofabrik extract works as well as one filtered by `osmium tags-filter`. A change to the speeds or to which ways are roads bumps `ENGINE_VERSION` (`engine/AGENTS.md`).
- Coordinates come from the ways when the file carries them (`osmium add-locations-to-ways`), otherwise from the file's nodes. An extract lacks nodes its ways refer to past its edge, so `add-locations-to-ways` runs with `--ignore-missing-nodes`: a road loses its segments to such a node, and a boundary with one is incomplete.
- A zone is a relation with `boundary=administrative`, `admin_level=2` and `ISO3166-1:alpha3`, or one of `SPECIAL_ZONES` in `src/zones.ts`, the cases of #13 by relation id (the world build's `osmium tags-filter` keeps those too). A part of a country (a closed territory, Northern Cyprus) comes before the countries in the graph's zones, so it wins where it lies inside its country's polygon. A zone is made of the rings of its relation that the extract holds whole; a ring with a way or a node the extract lacks is left out, and the report lists the zone as partial (the world's USA: its boundary around Kingman Reef and Johnston Atoll is in no part of Geofabrik's). A relation with no whole ring but holes (a neighbour at the edge of a country's extract) is left out and reported: its roads get no zone.
- An edge across a border is marked when it passes within 2 km of a `barrier=border_control` node (`src/border-controls.ts`). That mark is all the graph holds of borders: the engine applies the border table in `rules` on every calculation, so a change to the table needs no new graph.
- The build checks the border table first, and its report (`src/border-report.ts`) lists the pairs with passport control whose roads cross nowhere at a border crossing, and the table's open crossings near no road across their border. Both mean a crossing to add to or fix in the table. `src/build-report.ts` writes the report as Markdown, in Russian, for the world build's summary.
- Sized for the world, ~330 M road nodes before collapsing, in typed arrays outside the JS heap (`src/column.ts`, `src/node-ids.ts`). No node is kept by its id but the ones that stay in the graph. The first pass over the ways (`src/scan.ts`) puts every road node's id in `RepeatedIds` (`src/repeated-ids.ts`: sorted chunks, 8 bytes an id), which finds the ones at a junction. The second cuts each way into pieces at those, at its ends, at a node next to one the file lacks and wherever `collapse` keeps a node (`src/pieces.ts`); only the pieces' end nodes get a number. `collapse` (`src/collapse.ts`) then joins the pieces across ways into edges. Without locations on the ways, the road nodes' coordinates are kept by id (`src/osm-nodes.ts`), as for a country's raw extract.
- A road node's zone comes from `ZoneCells` (`src/zone-cells.ts`): once per 0.05° cell no zone boundary crosses, by `ZoneLocator` in the cells one does. `docs/world-build.md` has the world's numbers.

## Zone outlines

`zoneOutlines(graph, report.parts)` in `src/outlines.ts` turns the graph's zone polygons into the GeoJSON the screen hatches the closed countries and territories with (#30): a `FeatureCollection` with one `MultiPolygon` feature per zone, its code in `properties.zone`, [lon, lat] to 0.001°.

- Each ring is simplified by Douglas–Peucker to 2 km, and a ring smaller than 2 × 2 km after that (a small island) is left out.
- An outline is the zone as the graph's `ZoneLocator` sees it: the zone's rings by the even-odd rule (`polygon-clipping`'s `xor`), and the parts of countries (`report.parts`: closed territories, Northern Cyprus) cut out of the countries around them, since a part wins. So Northern Cyprus, open for Russians, is not hatched with Cyprus. Countries that overlap in OSM overlap here too.
- OSM's country boundaries run along the territorial waters, so coasts are smooth: Greece with its islands is 14 KB (3 KB in brotli), Cyprus with Northern Cyprus 3 KB; the world should be about 1 MB in brotli.
- The Worker serves the file as a static asset at `/data/zones.json`: `build-graph --outlines` writes it into `web/public/data/` (gitignored), which the client's build copies into `web/dist`.

## Cities file

`buildCities` in `src/cities.ts` makes the cities file the screen picks the cities in the ring from (#32, [spec #20](../docs/specs/20.md) «Файл городов», research in #6): JSON in the format of `src/cities-file.ts` (exported as `data/cities-file`, which the client reads), `{ format: 1, cities: [[id, ru, local, lat, lon, population, zone], …] }`. `encodeCities` writes the rows in order of id, each id as its difference from the row before's, which takes the world's file from 0.69 MB to 0.58 MB in brotli; `decodeCities` reads them back biggest first, from the file `parseCitiesFile` checks after `JSON.parse`.

- The cities are GeoNames' `cities1000` from 15 000 people, without PPLX (sections of cities), PPLH, PPLQ, PPLW (historical, abandoned, destroyed) and the parts of a city (below). The world on 2026-11-07, before the parts were left out: 31 795 cities, 21 097 of them with a Russian name (17 756 GeoNames', 3 341 Wikidata's), 0.60 MB in brotli, built in 98 s, Wikidata's 36 queries included (none answered 429).
- A city's limits (GLOSSARY «Пределы города») are a circle around its point of 9 km × √(its people in millions): Sarajevo's 7.5 km, Belgrade's 10, Moscow's 29. `limitsKm`, `distanceKm` and `cityAround` (the city whose limits hold a point, the nearest of several) are in `src/cities-file.ts`, which the screen reads too, so the builder and the screen draw the limits alike.
- A part of a city (GLOSSARY «Часть города», #45) is a place within the limits of another one at least 3 times as big (`PART_RATIO`): a district, Zemun and Novi Beograd in Belgrade, or a satellite grown into a city, Khimki in Moscow. GeoNames marks neither (Zemun is PPLA3, as are towns of their own), nor does Wikidata, so the rule is ours, with no list of exceptions yet: one comes with the first complaint. The parts are left out of the file whole (the format stays), before the Russian names and the zones; GeoNames' cities of 2026-11 have 2 941 of them, the biggest Pudong in Shanghai, and Brazzaville counts as Kinshasa's. Each place meets only the places whose limits touch its cell of 0.5° (`limitsGrid`), so this takes about a second for the world. A part holds no parts: a place in the limits of Novi Beograd but outside those of Belgrade stays a city. The report counts them and names the 20 biggest with their city (the nearest of those they are a part of) and the distance.
- `ru` is GeoNames' Russian name (from `alternateNamesV2`: in use, non-colloquial, Cyrillic, preferred, short, shortest, in that order), else Wikidata's Russian label of the item with that GeoNames id (P1566, `src/wikidata.ts`: 400 ids a query, one query at a time, waiting as long as a 429 says), else empty. `local` is GeoNames' `name`. That is the file in R2; the deploy then puts the table of Russian names over it (below).
- The table of Russian names, `russian-names.tsv` (#130): `geonameid\tname\tru` by GeoNames' id, the header first, a city's Russian name where GeoNames and Wikidata have none (Nogoyá, Javānrūd) or a broken one («Маронdera»). The deploy's `russian-names` (below) puts its names in the cities file `fetch-dataset` placed, over GeoNames' and Wikidata's, so an edit of the table goes live with the next merge to `main`, with no new dataset. `readRussianNames` (`src/russian-names.ts`, with its reading, applying and counting) checks it as it reads it: three columns, a positive integer id, no repeated id, a name of Russian letters, digits, space, hyphen, dot, brackets and apostrophe from a letter or a digit. A bad table fails the deploy, and the world build's `build-cities`, which reads it for its report. 9 431 rows of 2026-11 cover the world's cities without a Russian name (9 394) and with a broken one (37).
- Topping up the table: `build-cities`' log and report count the cities without a Russian name that the table lacks and name the 10 biggest. Haiku subagents in a Claude Code session write the missing names, a country to each, by the practical transcription of the country's language, from the rows `id`, `name`, country. The rows go in by id; `pnpm --filter data russian-names <a copy of a cities file>` then checks the table (`readRussianNames`) and names its first bad line, and `build-cities` counts what is still missing. A person reads what no check sees: letters of other alphabets that look Russian («Моррињуш» → «Морриньюс»), «ъ» outside Chinese and Japanese names, a lowercase first letter («аль-Хафджи» → «Аль-Хафджи»), a word added to the name («город Солнечногорск»). The table is edited by hand too: a wrong transcription is one line.
- `zone` is the graph's: the zone polygon the city is in, as `ZoneLocator` sees it, so Kyrenia is `NX` and Sukhumi `ABKHAZIA` though GeoNames puts them in CY and GE. A city outside every polygon (the West Bank has none in OSM) gets its GeoNames country as alpha-3 (`rules/countries`), and one whose country is no zone is left out and reported.
- The Worker serves the file as a static asset at `/data/cities.json`: `build-cities` writes it into `web/public/data/` (gitignored) for `vite dev` and local builds, like the zone outlines; `russian-names` then puts the table's names in.
- Other docs describe the cities file too, and a change to it updates them: `web/README.md` (`cities.ts`, the local file), `docs/world-build.md` (the job `build`), `docs/attribution.md` (GeoNames), `docs/specs/20-amendments.md` («Файл городов»).

## Places file

`buildPlaces` in `src/places.ts` makes the places file (spec #154 «Сборка мест», the amendment «Набор данных» of [spec #20](../docs/specs/20.md), ADR 0003): the places (GLOSSARY «Место») the screen puts in the ring beside the cities. JSON in the format of `src/places-file.ts` (exported as `data/places-file`, for the client), `{ format: 1, places: [[qid, ru, local, lat, lon, sitelinks, type, accessLat, accessLon, accessZone, …], …] }`: the Q-id as a number and as its difference from the row before's, as the cities' ids, `type` the index in `PLACE_TYPES`, the access points a flat tail of three values each. `encodePlaces` writes the rows in order of Q-id; `decodePlaces` reads them back as objects, the most famous first (`mostFamousFirst`: by sitelinks, then the smaller Q-id), from the file `parsePlacesFile` checks.

- The module also holds the 11 types in their order of precedence with their group, nature or history (`PLACE_GROUP`), and the fame key that puts cities and places in one list (GLOSSARY «Известность»): `placeFame`, sitelinks × `PLACE_WEIGHT`, and `cityFame`, `CITY_FAME_SCALE` · population^`CITY_FAME_EXPONENT`.
- The places are the objects of OSM with a type's tags (`TYPE_TAGS` in `src/places.ts`, the amendment's table) and `wikidata=`: nodes, closed ways and relations (a multipolygon, a national park's boundary); an open way is no area and is not read. Of several types, the first in `PLACE_TYPES`. A place of worship is a monastery only when Wikidata's item is an instance of Q44613, and otherwise of its other type, or no place. One place per item: of its objects, the one of the type that comes first, then an outline before a node (a relation's before a way's), then of the smaller id in OSM.
- A place has `MIN_SITELINKS` sitelinks or more, in every project of Wikimedia, counted in Wikidata's dump of `wb_items_per_site` (`src/sitelinks.ts`), which also gives the title of its article in ruwiki.
- The outlines (`src/place-outlines.ts`) are read once the sitelinks have kept the famous items: a pass over the file's ways for theirs, and over its nodes when the ways carry no locations. A relation's ways are joined into rings as a zone's (`relationRings` of `src/zones.ts`), its `inner` ways the holes; one the file holds no whole outer ring of is left out. An outline over `MAX_SQUARE_KM` (the outer rings less the holes) is a region, not a place: Crete, Ladoga. Each is kept simplified by Douglas–Peucker to `OUTLINE_TOLERANCE_METERS` (`simplifyRing` of `src/outlines.ts`), to 0.001°, as `PlaceOutline` of the format module.
- A place is out of every city's limits (GLOSSARY «Пределы города», `cityAround` on the cities file, by `limitsGrid` of `src/cities.ts`): a node by its point, an outline unless it lies wholly in one city's limits; the place itself counts, not its access points.
- Its access points (GLOSSARY «Подъезд», `src/access-points.ts`) are on roads with both ends in one zone, as the origin's (`engine/src/snap.ts`); to 0.001°, as the ring holds the land 1 km around its roads. A node's are the nearest point of a road in each zone within `MAX_ACCESS_METERS`, the nearest first. An outline's are on a grid of cells of `ACCESS_GRID_METERS`, rows from the equator and columns as wide at the middle of their row: in each cell and zone, the point of a road nearest the cell's middle of those in the cell and inside the outline or within `ACCESS_EDGE_METERS` of its edge, the cells south to north, then west to east: a road whose point nearest the middle is out of the outline is taken where it comes within `ACCESS_EDGE_METERS` of an edge (`nearestTaken`, on the plane tangent at the cell's middle). An outline with no such road gets a node's access points from a point inside it. A place without one is left out. The grid's step is the lever of the file's size against its budget. The roads near the places are found by `RoadIndex` (`src/road-index.ts`), a grid of the roads near the given points only, which the Valhalla comparison uses for its cities too.
- After the sitelinks, the cities and the roads, Wikidata's Query Service is asked for the items left, 400 a query (`wikidataPlaces` of `src/wikidata.ts`): the Russian and English labels, P625 and Q44613. `ru` is the Russian label, else ruwiki's title without its qualifier in brackets («Д'Арк (гора)»), from a capital letter, else empty; `local` is the English label, else OSM's `name`. A place's point is its node's, or for an outline P625, else a point inside it (the middle of its widest span on the middle line of its biggest ring's box). That is the file in R2; the deploy then puts the table of Russian names of places over it (below).
- The table of Russian names of places, `place-russian-names.tsv` (#158): `qid\tname\tru` by Q-id (`Q6386`), the header first, a place's Russian name, over Wikidata's label and ruwiki's title, where they have none or a poor one. The deploy's `russian-names` (below) puts its names in the places file, as the cities' table in the cities file, so an edit of it goes live with the next merge to `main`, with no new dataset. `readPlaceRussianNames` (`src/russian-names.ts`, beside the cities' table, with `withPlaceRussianNames`) checks it as the cities' table: three columns, a key `Q` and a positive integer, no repeated Q-id, a name of the same Russian letters and signs; a bad table fails the deploy, naming its line, and `build-places`, which reads it for its report. It starts with its header only; #164 fills it.
- Topping up the table: `build-places`' log and report count the places without a Russian name that the table lacks and name the 10 most famous. Haiku subagents write the missing names, as for the cities (#164), from the rows `qid`, `local`, the type and the country: a generic word is translated («Озеро Гарда»), a proper name transcribed by the practical transcription of the country's language. The rows go in by Q-id; `pnpm --filter data russian-names <a copy of a places file>` then checks the table and names its first bad line, and `build-places` counts what is still missing. A person reads what no check sees, as for the cities.
- The cells of the outlines, for the origin's places (GLOSSARY «Место точки старта»): the outlines of the places from `MIN_OUTLINE_METERS` across (the longer side of their box), in cells of 1° × 1°, each in every cell the boxes of its edges touch with `ORIGIN_PLACE_METERS` to spare (`outlineCells` of `src/place-outlines.ts`), whole, not cut at the cell. A cell is a file of the format module, `{ format: 1, outlines: [[qid, [lat, lon, lat, lon, …], …], …] }` in order of Q-id, each ring flat and not closed, a point in the place by the even-odd rule of its rings (`encodePlaceOutlines`, `decodePlaceOutlines`, `parsePlaceOutlinesFile`). The Worker serves them at `/data/place-outlines/<lat>_<lon>.json` by the cell's south-west corner (`PLACE_OUTLINES_PATH`; `placeOutlinesPath` of a point gives its cell's file, `/data/place-outlines/44_20.json` for Belgrade, `-34_-71` for Santiago). Only a cell an outline touches has a file: for the client, a cell answered 404 is one with no outline.
- The rule of the origin's places is `originPlaces(origin, places, outlines)` of the format module, which the screen uses too: of the places of the file and the outlines of the origin's cell, those whose outline holds the origin or is nearer it than `ORIGIN_PLACE_METERS` (`nearOutline`); any other place is a point, an origin's place nearer than `ORIGIN_PLACE_METERS` for a mountain, a waterfall or a cave, and than that and half `MIN_OUTLINE_METERS` for the other types, which may be a small outline the file has no size of.
- The report (`PlacesReport`) counts the objects read, those left out at each step, the places, the cells of the outlines with the outlines in them and their bytes in all, and the places without a Russian name that the table lacks, with the most famous of them.
- The Worker serves the file as a static asset at `/data/places.json` (`PLACES_PATH`), beside the cities file, and the cells under `/data/place-outlines/`: the world build's job `places` builds them (below), `fetch-dataset` places them in `web/public/data/`, and the deploy's `russian-names` then names the places. A dataset without places (one from before #159) puts neither there, and the screen shows the cities only.

## Commands

`pnpm --filter data ring <extract | graph file | URL> --lat <degrees> --lon <degrees> --hours <N> [--from <hours>] [--closed <alpha-3,…>] [--out <file.geojson>]` is the one-command check of the ticket: it builds the graph of an extract (for example `https://download.geofabrik.de/europe/andorra-latest.osm.pbf`), computes the ring (N − 1, N], or (`--from`, N] for a ring of days, and writes GeoJSON. `build-graph <extract> <out.graph> [--outlines <zones.json>]` writes the graph file, which `ring` also reads, and with `--outlines` the zone outlines, printing their size raw and in brotli. Both print the build report to stderr when they build. pnpm runs both in `data/`; they take a relative path from where pnpm started.

`build-graph … --report <report.md>` also writes the build report as Markdown.

`ring` takes a negative coordinate either way, `--lon -3.7` or `--lon=-3.7`.

Rings on real roads, as a ticket of the engine asks for pictures before and after:

- `region <out.graph> <part | URL>… [--mirror <url>]` downloads the parts (paths in Geofabrik's layout, `europe/austria`), keeps what the world build keeps of them (the job `extract` below), merges them and writes the graph file. It needs osmium-tool on PATH (`apt-get install osmium-tool`).
- `render <out.png> <label=ring.geojson>…` draws rings `ring --out` wrote side by side, on one scale, as the screen draws them, and prints what each is made of: polygons, holes, points, the GeoJSON's size, the contour's time. It uses `@playwright/test`'s Chromium.
- On the world graph, the current dataset in R2, which no session can download: the workflow `.github/workflows/world-rings.yml` on a branch's code. A cloud session starts it by pushing a change to `.github/world-rings.json` (`rings`: `<name> <lat> <lon> <from> <to>` separated by `;`, `closed`, `mode`); by hand, `gh workflow run world-rings.yml --ref <branch> -f rings='…'`. It puts the rings' GeoJSON in the artifact `world-rings` (`gh run download <run> -n world-rings`) for `render`; `mode=container` also times each ring in the Container's image under `standard-2`'s limits, a row per answer in the run's summary. Before and after: run it on `main` and on the branch.
- Write the rings of the old engine before you change it, then the new ones, and render both. A picture for a PR goes on an artifact page that its description links to: GitHub's API attaches no images.
- A cloud session reaches neither Geofabrik nor geo2day: both drop the connection. `--mirror https://download.openstreetmap.fr/extracts` serves Geofabrik's layout with `_` for a space (`europe/czech_republic`). Its `europe/` holds western and central Europe, Scandinavia, Poland, Czechia, Slovakia, Turkey and Ukraine, but no Hungary, Slovenia, the Balkans, Greece, Romania or the Baltics; its `asia/` holds Georgia and Armenia, but no Central Asia. A ticket that asks for rings on real roads picks its cities where the mirror has them: Vienna, Prague, Madrid, Oslo.

`publish-dataset <dataset directory> [--bucket <name>] [--prefix <prefix>] [--trial] [--places-failed]` puts a dataset in R2 (below); it needs the aws CLI, `CLOUDFLARE_ACCOUNT_ID` and R2's S3 credentials in `AWS_ACCESS_KEY_ID` and `AWS_SECRET_ACCESS_KEY`. The directory holds the graph's and the cities' files and the places': `places.json` and `place-outlines/`, as `build-places` writes them. Before it uploads anything, it checks that `zones.json` has a zone for every country of `COUNTRIES` but `ZONELESS_COUNTRIES` (`src/zoneless.ts`: the 35 without one in the world of 2026-11-07, each with the outline that holds its land) and names those that lack one; `--trial` skips that, for a dataset of some parts of the world. A country that loses its zone in OSM for good joins the list. `--places-failed` takes a directory without the places' files, when their job failed: the version gets the current version's, copied in the bucket, or none when it has none, and its `report.md` gets a section «Места» saying which. The cities have no such fallback: without them the build fails.

`fetch-dataset [--bucket <name>]` is the first step of the `roadtrip` Worker's deploy (`worker/README.md`, «Deploy»): `fetchDataset` (`src/fetch-dataset.ts`) reads the pointer, downloads its version's `graph.graph` and `dataset.json` into `engine/dataset/`, `zones.json`, `cities.json` and `places.json` into `web/public/data/`, each next to its place, and the cells of the outlines (`place-outlines.tsv`, below) into `web/public/data/place-outlines/`, a file per cell, in place of the cells there were. It moves them in only once every file's size and SHA-256 match the pointer's, so a bad download leaves the files there were. A version without the places' files puts none, which is no error. It reads R2 through Cloudflare's API with `CLOUDFLARE_API_TOKEN` (Workers R2 Storage: Read) and `CLOUDFLARE_ACCOUNT_ID`, which Workers Builds gives the deploy.

`build-cities <graph file | extract | URL> <out.json> [--cities <cities1000.zip | .txt | URL>] [--names <alternateNamesV2.zip | .txt | URL>,…] [--countries <alpha-2>,…] [--no-wikidata] [--report <report.md>]` writes the cities file with the zones of the graph's polygons and prints how many cities, names and zones it got, the cities without a Russian name that the table of Russian names lacks, the parts of a city it left out, and the size raw and in brotli. `--report` adds the parts of a city and the cities the table lacks to the build report that `build-graph --report` wrote (`cityPartsMarkdown` and `missingRussianNamesMarkdown` of `src/build-report.ts`). By default it downloads GeoNames' `cities1000.zip` and `alternateNamesV2.zip` (205 MB, streamed through `unzip -p`): that is the world build's call with the world graph. `--countries` keeps the cities of those GeoNames countries and takes their own files of alternate names (`alternatenames/<alpha-2>.zip`), for a region: `build-cities georgia.graph ../web/public/data/cities.json --countries GE,AM,AZ,TR` takes 5 s, Wikidata included. `--no-wikidata` skips Wikidata.

`build-places <places.osm.pbf | .osm> <graph file | extract | URL> <cities.json> <out.json> [--sitelinks <wb_items_per_site.sql.gz | URL>] [--no-wikidata] [--report <report.md>]` writes the places file (above) of the objects of OSM in the first file, the roads of the graph and the cities of a cities file, and the cells of the outlines beside it, in `place-outlines/` next to `<out.json>`, in place of those there were, as the Worker serves them beside the file. It prints how many places of how many objects, what it left out at each step, the places without a Russian name that the table of Russian names of places lacks and the most famous of them, the size raw and in brotli, and how many cells of how many places' outlines in how many bytes. Over `PLACES_BUDGET_BYTES` in brotli of quality 11 it fails and writes no file, nor any cell. `--report` adds the same to the build report (`placesMarkdown` of `src/build-report.ts`). By default it downloads Wikidata's latest dump of the sitelinks; a `.gz` file is read through gunzip. `--no-wikidata` skips the Query Service: no place of worship is then a monastery, and the names are ruwiki's titles and OSM's.

`russian-names [<cities.json | places.json | directory>…] [--names <table.tsv>] [--place-names <table.tsv>]` puts the tables of Russian names over the names of a cities file and of a places file in place, the deploy's step after `fetch-dataset`: the cities' table (by default the shipped `russian-names.tsv`) over a cities file, the places' table (by default the shipped `place-russian-names.tsv`) over a places file, each file known by what it holds. A directory stands for its `cities.json` and its `places.json` when it has one; by default `web/public/data/`, where a dataset without places has none, which is no error. It prints how many cities and places it named and how many of each table's keys the files lack; a bad table (both are read first) fails it, naming its line, before any file is touched.

`pnpm --filter data valhalla <work dir> [--report <file.md>] [--regions <id,…>] [--mirror <url>]` is the comparison with Valhalla on the full graph (#24; [spec #20](../docs/specs/20.md) «Сверка с Valhalla»), a one-off check that CI does not run. The start points and their regions are in `src/valhalla/places.ts`. It needs osmium-tool, unzip and pyvalhalla's commands on PATH, a few GB of disk per region and ~10 GB of memory while Valhalla builds a region's tiles. Valhalla's results stay in the work directory, so after a change to the speeds a second run only recomputes ours. Its last report is `valhalla-report.md`, with conclusions written by hand; rerun it after a change to the speeds or the contour, and keep the conclusions.

## The world build

`.github/workflows/dataset.yml` builds the world's dataset on the 2nd of each month ([spec #20](../docs/specs/20.md) «Набор данных», #14, #35), or when run by hand (`workflow_dispatch`: the extracts' date, the parts to take, and `parts_run_id`, a run whose filtered parts to build from instead of downloading them again), or as a trial on a push (below). Its jobs:

1. `plan` picks the date: the newest one every part's page at Geofabrik lists a dated extract of (`europe-261107.osm.pbf`). Geofabrik keeps those of the 1st of each month for months and the others for a week. The dataset's version is that date, `2026-11-07`; a rebuild of a published date's extracts after a fix of the build is started by hand with the input `revision` (2, 3, …) and publishes as `2026-11-07-r2`.
2. `extract`, one job per part of the world (Russia is none: the others cover it), downloads the part of that date, checks its MD5 and that it holds OSM of that date, then keeps with osmium what the builder reads: `tags-filter` of the roads from motorway to tertiary with their links, `barrier=border_control` and `admin_level=2` with their members, and `getid -r` of `SPECIAL_ZONES`. Apart from them, in the artifact `osm-places-<part>`, the objects that may be places: a `tags-filter` of `wikidata`, then of its output the keys and values of `TYPE_TAGS` (`src/places.ts`; a tag added there is added to this filter), each with its members and their nodes; `build-places` checks the rest.
3. `build` merges the parts (two parts of one date hold one version of an object; the job fails otherwise), runs `add-locations-to-ways --ignore-missing-nodes`, then `build-graph` with `--outlines` and `--report`, and `build-cities` on the graph, which adds the parts of a city and the cities the table of Russian names lacks to the build report. The build report goes to the run's summary, the dataset to an artifact kept 14 days.
4. `places` (spec #154 «Ежемесячная сборка и деплой») merges the places' parts and runs `build-places` on them, the graph and the cities file of `build`, with Wikidata's dump of the sitelinks and its Query Service; its part of the build report goes to the run's summary, the places file, the cells and that part to the artifact `places-<version>`. It fails over the places file's budget, over 19 000 cells (the Worker serves at most 20 000 static files, about a hundred of them the client's own), or when a part's run (`parts_run_id`) has no places' parts. Its failure stops nothing: `publish` runs without it.
5. `publish` puts the places' files beside the dataset's and their part of the report after the rest, or passes `--places-failed` when `places` failed, and puts the dataset in R2 with `publish-dataset` (`src/publish.ts`), with the S3 credentials R2 derives from the Cloudflare API token in the repository's secrets: its id, and the SHA-256 of its value. Then it deploys the new dataset: `.github/dataset/deploy.sh` starts the build of the Worker `roadtrip` in Cloudflare Workers Builds on the default branch, with the same token, and waits for the build's outcome, so a failed deploy fails the run (`docs/agents/infrastructure.md`). A trial deploys nothing.

Every heavy step runs under `.github/dataset/measure.sh`, which puts its time, peak memory and the peak disk the job's files take in the summary; `docs/world-build.md` has the numbers of a run.

The dataset in R2, bucket `roadtrip-datasets`:

- `<version>/graph.graph`, `zones.json`, `cities.json`, `dataset.json` (`{ "version": "<version>" }`, as `engine/dataset/dataset.json`) and `report.md` (the build report);
- `<version>/places.json` and `place-outlines.tsv`, the cells of the outlines in one file, a line per cell, `<cell>\t<its file>` in order of the cell's name (`src/place-outlines-bundle.ts`): thousands of objects would be thousands of requests to Cloudflare's API in the deploy, which answers 1 200 in 5 minutes. A version from before #159, or one whose places job failed with no places in the version before, has neither;
- `<version>/manifest.json`: the version and each file's size and SHA-256. Once it is there, the version keeps its files, so one version names one dataset: `publish-dataset` takes the same files again (a rerun) and refuses others before it uploads anything, which then go under a new version (`revision`, above);
- `current.json`: the pointer, a copy of the current version's manifest. It moves only once every file of the version is uploaded and its size checked, so a failed run leaves the version before it current. The deploy reads it (`fetch-dataset`, above), then takes the files under its version.

A run of only some of the parts is a trial: it publishes under `trials/<run id>/` with `--trial` and deletes that at the end, so the current dataset stays. A cloud session, which may not dispatch a workflow, starts one by pushing a change to `.github/dataset-trial.json` (`parts`, and optionally `date` and `parts_run_id`) on a branch other than `main`: a run a push starts is a trial whatever parts it names, and deploys nothing. Its R2 objects are gone at its end; its artifacts (`dataset-<version>`, `places-<version>`) keep what it built, `gh run download <run> -n places-<version>`.

## Tests

The fixtures in `test/fixtures/` are hand-written OSM XML of a few countries and tens of nodes near the equator, where 0.1° is 11.1 km. Each one's header works out the expected values by hand; a new case extends a fixture's header or gets its own fixture.

- `fixtureGraph` (`test/fixture-graph.ts`) builds a whole fixture and fails when a way refers to a node the file lacks: the builder would drop that road's segments without a word. A cut extract (`edge-of-extract.osm`, `partial-zone.osm`) goes through `buildGraph` itself.
- The builder collapses a chain of nodes no other road meets into one straight edge, so a road's bend holds only where a third road meets it: a square loop of road keeps its corners only with a stub of road at each (`plots.osm`). A road that ends at a corner of a loop is such a chain too, its end and the loop's side one diagonal edge: run it on past the corner (`far-loop.osm`).
- `test/ring.test.ts`: the engine's seam ([spec #20](../docs/specs/20.md) «Решения по тестам», seam 1), borders included. Its border tests build their own small tables with `test/rules.ts`; the closed territories and the zones of #13 use the shipped table.
- `test/build-graph.test.ts`: the graph itself, the PBF reader against the XML, the graph file, the build report.
- `test/outlines.test.ts`: the zone outlines against the graph's `ZoneLocator` on `zones.osm`, and the simplification.
- `test/border-table.test.ts`, `test/data-version.test.ts` and `test/engine-fingerprint.test.ts`: the border table's check, the data version and the engine's fingerprint (`engine/AGENTS.md`; `src/engine-fingerprint.ts`), here because every package's tests run under `data`'s vitest and types.
- `test/cities.test.ts`: the cities file from lines in GeoNames' formats, a double of Wikidata and the zones of `zones.osm`, the parts of a city on real places of GeoNames around Belgrade, Sarajevo and Moscow; `test/wikidata.test.ts`: the Wikidata client against a double of the Query Service, for the cities and for the places.
- `test/places.test.ts`: the places file from `places.osm` (its header has the places, the roads and the distances), a tiny dump of the sitelinks (`test/sitelinks-dump.ts`, in the dump's own layout), a double of Wikidata and two cities; the places with an outline, their access points on the grid and the cells from `place-outlines.osm`, whose header works out the grid's cells by hand; `test/places-file.test.ts`: the format, the fame key, the cells' files and the rule of the origin's places.
- `test/commands.test.ts`: the commands as processes; `build-cities` reads the small GeoNames files in `test/fixtures/geonames/` and the shipped table of Russian names (Nogoyá is in it), `russian-names` small cities and places files, tables of its own and the shipped table, and directories with and without a places file, `build-places` `places.osm` with a gzipped dump, `place-outlines.osm` for the cells it writes, and a file of random names over the budget.
- `test/zone-locator.test.ts`: `ZoneLocator` and the builder's `ZoneCells` on squares of whole degrees.
- `test/publish.test.ts`: `publishDataset` against a double of R2: the pointer moves last, and not at all after a failed upload; nothing goes over a version published with other files, nor anywhere when a country lost its zone, but in a trial; the places file and the cells in the version and the pointer, and with `placesFailed` the version before's, or none, with the report saying which; `ZONELESS_COUNTRIES` names countries of `COUNTRIES` only.
- `test/fetch-dataset.test.ts`: `fetchDataset` against a double of R2: the files of the pointer's version take their places, the cells a file each in their directory, and none does when one's SHA-256 is not the pointer's or the bucket lacks it; a version without places puts none.
- `test/valhalla-metrics.test.ts`: the measures of the Valhalla comparison (ring area, the median time to cities, the tolerance); the comparison itself runs in no test.
